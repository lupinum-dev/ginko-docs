## Feature: at the apiary

<Flow>
**Vulkanrot · In the garden**

### One place, many perspectives

Red beehives stand in the evening light. Flowers grow in front of them; the garden continues behind them. A wide photograph establishes the setting before we move closer.

<Figure src="/images/editorial/hives.webp" alt="Red beehives among flowering plants in the evening sun" caption="The apiary in the evening sun. Photograph: Imkerei Vulkanrot." bleed="outside" aspect="video" frame="none" />

A quiet passage gives the photograph room. Lines stay at a readable width on a large screen. The next image can expand again without putting each paragraph in its own column.

<Excerpt size="display" label="Design principle">
Show the place first. Then move closer.
</Excerpt>

A feature alternates between context and observation. Text guides readers through the story. Captions identify what the photographs show.
</Flow>

## Nature magazine: text around an image

<Flow width="wide">
### Stay with the detail

<Figure src="/images/editorial/bee.webp" alt="Honeybee on yellow cornelian cherry blossoms" caption="A close view of a honeybee. Photo: Imkerei Vulkanrot." placement="end" frame="none" aspect="square" />

The bee rests on a yellow flower. In a close photograph, a small detail becomes the main subject. Text starts beside it and returns to the full line width below the image.

This arrangement suits observations, short profiles, and background passages. The photograph stays near the passage that discusses it. Its caption belongs to the same unit. On a phone, both appear before the text that follows them in the document.

Use enough text to make the wrapping useful. A short passage beside a tall photograph leaves a gap. A square crop and a brief caption keep this example compact. The text can continue through several paragraphs without a manual column break.

The next paragraph continues the story. There is no second text box and no forced line break at the bottom of the photograph. When the font size or screen width changes, the text flows again. A new heading begins below the photograph, so the next section has a clear starting point.
</Flow>

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem title="Syntax: text wrapping">
```mdc [content/docs/magazine.md]
<Flow width="wide">
<Figure src="/images/editorial/bee.webp" alt="Bee on a blossom" caption="Photo: Vulkanrot." placement="end" frame="none" aspect="square" />

Several paragraphs start beside the image and continue below it.
</Flow>
```
</AccordionItem>

## Technical magazine: text and a margin note

<Layout gap="lg" stack="md">
<Column size="lg">
### One idea, two levels of detail

The main text introduces the subject. A margin note defines a term, names a source, or adds an observation. The passage stays readable, with supporting information beside it.

Keep each note beside a short section. On a narrow screen, the note follows that section immediately. The next idea starts after the note.
</Column>

<Column size="sm">
<Aside label="In the margin · 01" appearance="tint">
**Captions and alternative text**

Everyone can see the caption. Alternative text describes the image for people who cannot see it.
</Aside>
</Column>
</Layout>

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem title="Syntax: a margin note with existing components">
```mdc [content/docs/magazine.md]
<Layout gap="lg">
<Column size="lg">
A short main passage.
</Column>

<Column size="sm">
<Aside label="In the margin" appearance="tint">
The supporting explanation.
</Aside>
</Column>
</Layout>
```
</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>
