Business websites often need more than one hero image. A launch recap, event
story, studio update, or client field note may combine single images, short
image rows, and a larger gallery. This article is the blog example for those
cases.

## A Normal Inline Image

Use a regular Markdown image when the image belongs in the reading flow and
does not need a caption or bleed treatment.

![Bright workspace prepared for an editorial planning session](/images/content-demo-workspace.jpg)

## A Single Captioned Figure

Use a figure when the image needs explicit dimensions, a caption, or a stable
aspect ratio. Dimensions avoid layout shift; alt text explains the image for
readers who cannot see it.

:::figure{src="/images/content-demo-detail.png" alt="Detailed planning material for a website content session" caption="A single figure works well for product details, process screenshots, and editorial proof points." width="1200" height="800" aspect="video"}
:::

## A Wide Editorial Moment

Use a bleed figure when the image is important enough to break out from the
normal text measure on larger screens. It stays aligned on mobile.

:::figure{src="/images/content-demo-workshop.jpg" alt="Workshop table with notes for a multilingual website launch" caption="Bleed media gives important editorial images more presence without creating a separate template." width="1200" height="900" bleed=true aspect="video"}
:::

## Production Rule

Keep images with the page that owns them. If a client deletes the article, its
unused images should be deleted too, and the asset check should fail when a
public image no longer has a route or content owner.
